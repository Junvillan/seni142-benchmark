\section{Multi-Temporal AC-OPF with Multi-Vector Coupling}
\label{sec:opf}

The multi-temporal operational campaign exercises the 215-contingency $N-1$ security, two-layer reserve sizing, and bounded-slack framework established in Part~I~\cite{seni142part1}, coupling it directly to multi-vector storage and flexible conversion assets.


\subsection{Multi-temporal AC-OPF with power-to-gas coupling}
\label{sub:opf}

On the converged network model, the benchmark formulates a multi-temporal AC optimal power flow \cite{molzahn2019}. The dispatch prices energy and both reserve directions,
\ifCLASSOPTIONonecolumn
\begin{equation}
\min \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}} \left[ \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}} \left( c_g^{P}(P_{g,t}) + c_g^{+} r_{g,t}^{+} + c_g^{-} r_{g,t}^{-} \right) + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}} c^{\mathrm{shed}}\, P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{shed}} \right],
\label{eq:opfobj}
\end{equation}
\else
\begin{multline}
\min \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}} \left[ \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}} \left( c_g^{P}(P_{g,t}) + c_g^{+} r_{g,t}^{+} + c_g^{-} r_{g,t}^{-} \right) \right. \\
\left. + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}} c^{\mathrm{shed}}\, P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{shed}} \right],
\label{eq:opfobj}
\end{multline}
\fi
subject to the AC balances, the thermal branch limits, the apparent-power disk of Part~I~\cite{seni142part1}, the hourly ramps $|P_{g,t} - P_{g,t-1}| \le \Delta_g^{\mathrm{ramp}}$, and the storage balances of pumped hydro and hydrogen.

The shedding term is an exact penalty: $c^{\mathrm{shed}}$ is fixed strictly above any achievable system marginal cost, $c^{\mathrm{shed}} > \max_{g \in \mathcal{G}} \partial c_g^{P} / \partial P_{g,t}$.

\begin{proposition}[Zero unserved load at qualified local optima]
\label{prop:shed}
Let the AC-OPF admit a local minimizer satisfying: (i) the linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ); (ii) static feasibility of the post-event network, i.e.\ there exists a dispatch $x \in \mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{post}}$ with $g(x) = 0$, $h(x) \le 0$, and $P^{\mathrm{shed}} = 0$; (iii) bounded transmission-congestion rents, such that the nodal energy multiplier satisfies $\lambda_{i,t} \le \lambda^{\max} < c^{\mathrm{shed}} = 10{,}000\ \text{\$}/\mathrm{MWh}$ uniformly across all admissible post-contingency dispatches. Then the local minimizer carries $P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{shed}} = 0$ at every bus and hour.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Under LICQ, KKT stationarity in $P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{shed}}$ reads $c^{\mathrm{shed}} = \lambda_{i,t} + \mu_{i,t}$, with $\lambda_{i,t}$ the multiplier of the nodal balance \eqref{eq:opf_balance} and $\mu_{i,t} \ge 0$ the multiplier of the nonnegativity bound $P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{shed}} \ge 0$. By hypothesis (iii), $\lambda_{i,t} \le \lambda^{\max} < c^{\mathrm{shed}}$; unserved load positive at optimality would instead force $\mu_{i,t} = 0$ by complementarity and hence $\lambda_{i,t} = c^{\mathrm{shed}}$ --- a contradiction. Dual feasibility therefore gives $\mu_{i,t} = c^{\mathrm{shed}} - \lambda_{i,t} \ge 10{,}000 - 350 = 9{,}650\ \text{\$}/\mathrm{MWh} > 0$ strictly and uniformly in the admissible congestion level, and complementarity forces $P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{shed}} = 0$ identically.
\end{IEEEproof}

Hypothesis (iii) is an empirical ceiling, not a derived consequence: the highest registered marginal generation cost ($230\ \text{\$}/\mathrm{MWh}$) plus worst-case observed transmission-congestion rents ($120\ \text{\$}/\mathrm{MWh}$) place the admissible multiplier scale below $350\ \text{\$}/\mathrm{MWh}$ across the validation campaign. The statement is deliberately local and conditional: KKT conditions are necessary at qualified local minimizers, not certificates of global optimality on this nonconvex problem, and hypothesis (ii) is exactly the per-contingency condition that clause 4 of the PASS battery of Part~I~\cite{seni142part1} verifies.

For each hour $t$ and bus $i$, the equality constraints are the nodal balances, augmented with the power-to-gas coupling terms,
\ifCLASSOPTIONonecolumn
\begin{equation}
\sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}_i} P_{g,i,t} + \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_i} P_{r,i,t} + P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{FC}} - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{EL}} + P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{shed}} - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{load}} = P_i(\mathbf{V}_t, \boldsymbol{\theta}_t),
\label{eq:opf_balance}
\end{equation}
\else
\begin{multline}
\sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}_i} P_{g,i,t} + \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_i} P_{r,i,t} + P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{FC}} - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{EL}} \\
+ P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{shed}} - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{load}} = P_i(\mathbf{V}_t, \boldsymbol{\theta}_t),
\label{eq:opf_balance}
\end{multline}
\fi
with the reactive analogue admitting inverter-coupled absorption/injection within apparent-power capability limits. The intertemporal coupling is imposed by the hydrogen buffer dynamics,
\begin{equation}
E_{h,t+1} = E_{h,t} + \left(\eta_{\mathrm{EL}}\, P_{h,t}^{\mathrm{EL}} - \frac{P_{h,t}^{\mathrm{FC}}}{\eta_{\mathrm{FC}}}\right) \Delta t,
\label{eq:h2_storage}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
E_h^{\min} \le E_{h,t} \le E_h^{\max},
\label{eq:h2_limits}
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\mathrm{EL}}$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{FC}}$ are the electrolysis and reconversion efficiencies: surplus non-dispatchable renewable generation becomes intertemporal energy inventory, absorbed as flexible electrolytic load in surplus hours and re-injected by the fuel cell in deficit hours. Pumped storage obeys the reservoir analogue with independent pump/turbine efficiencies, bounding the reversible 764~MW fleet of Section~\ref{sub:phes}. The complete problem is nonconvex because the AC equalities are nonconvex. It is solved by a cascade of thirty-two Newton-type configurations spanning Newton--Raphson \cite{tinney1967}, Iwamoto optimal-multiplier step control, fast-decoupled BX iterations \cite{stott1974}, and a Gauss--Seidel fallback, so that no single algebraic pathology can stall the sweep. The Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) system certifies local stationarity only; on this nonconvex problem it is not a certificate of global optimality (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:shed}). Stationarity of the Lagrangian, primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementarity are archived with the sealed validation output \cite{nocedal2006}, \cite{dommel1968}, \cite{wood2013}. Figure~\ref{fig:p2g_h2} diagrams the electrochemical coupling.
